## The Temporal Imaginary of Seventh-day Adventism

- framework of imaginary

- distinguish SDA imaginary from the imaginary of Finney and of Darby (later)


The statue of Nebuchadnezzar's dream, interpreted by Daniel as portraying to progression (and devolution) of the kingdoms to follow after Babylon, appears frequently in explorations of end-times events. The golden head of head of Babylon is followed by the silver of "Medo-Persia", and the bronze of "Greece". Next the iron legs of the Roman empire, ending in the iron and clay feet and toes of the "divided kingdom," which are parsed by later interpreters as the ten kingdoms (of ...). Each kingdom would overthrow the next until finally all would be replaced by the kingdom of heaven. 

This progression of events is held to be repeated in the four beasts of Daniel 7. Here the lion represents Babylon, followed by the bear of the Medo-Persian empire, the leopard of the Greeks, and the beast of the Romans. As with the ten toes of Nebuchadnezzar's statue, the beast of Daniel 7 features ten horns, and grows an eleventh, representing the last kingdom preceding the kingdom of heaven.
Finally, the Ram and Goat of Daniel 8 again represent the clash between the Medo-Persian empire and the Greeks. For the Seventh-day Adventist commentators, the breaking of the horn of the ram referred to the death of Alexander the great, the appearance of four horns in its place to the division of power between four military leaders, and the growth of the "little horn" with the rise of the Papacy and the "Union of Church and State."^[@Sadler1905, p. 19, 20]

All of this interpretive work depends on assumptions of the literal accuracy of the biblical accounts - the power of prophecy derived from it being "history in advance" - shown to be trust worthy in predicting events before they arrived. The historical-critical methods of biblical scholarship since Miller has complicated the history of these texts and their prophetic pronouncements. Such methods, not surprisingly, have been broadly rejected by Seventh-day Adventists and other fundamentalist Protestant groups.

Rather than envisioning time as a progressing in regular intervals, I am envisioning time for Seventh-day Adventists as a sound wave, with periods of compression, or heightened expectation when the second coming seems near, and rarefaction, or periods of expansion when the second coming seems further away.
