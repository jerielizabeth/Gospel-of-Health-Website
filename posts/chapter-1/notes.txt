One of the reoccurring feature of American religious life and practice has been the revival and millennial anticipation. From the Puritan's dream of a "City on a Hill" to the Left Behind series and the May 21st movement of the current day, American Protestants have sought to understand themselves and their history in terms of redemptive history, as living in the "last days" of God's redemptive plan for humanity.

However, while this undercurrent of millennialism, closely tied to American exceptionalism, has a robust presence in US history, the particular formulations have changed and shifted over time, reflecting the cultural and social realities of believers. 

Seventh-day Adventism offers a third option for understanding the religious and cultural trends of the nineteenth-century. For much of its early history, Seventh-day Adventism occupied a precarious space on the edges of "respectable" evangelical practice.
